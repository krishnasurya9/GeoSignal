# GeoSignal Research Status

## Intended system and paper claim

GeoSignal is proposed as an architecture for live geopolitical monitoring: ingest
events and reporting as they emerge, connect them to relevant actors and
economic entities, and surface potentially affected sectors or securities with
market context. The paper evaluates this intended workflow by replaying
historical data through the existing prototype. A live deployment is not needed
for this offline evaluation.

The defensible claim at this stage is that the prototype can process historical
geopolitical event records and news metadata, establish traceable source links,
generate additional lexical candidates, and construct descriptive actor and
market context. The current case does not establish reliable sector/stock
impact identification, predictive accuracy, or live operational performance.

## Historical data and run

- The local archive contains 91 daily GDELT event exports and 91 GDELT GKG
  metadata exports for 2024 Q1.
- The completed case is the Red Sea / Houthi event sample dated 2024-01-12.
- Thirty GDELT event records were selected; the same-day GKG file contributed
  108,343 usable metadata records. GKG v1 metadata is not full article text.
- Exact normalized source-URL joins produced 31 event/article rows covering all
  30 selected events. This is source identity linkage, not semantic validation.
- BM25/TF-IDF produced 230 additional candidate rows for 25 of 30 events
  (83.33% candidate-generation coverage).
- Against exact source URLs as a silver-standard linkage signal, lexical
  candidates contained one exact source pair: 0.43% of candidate rows, and the
  exact source appeared in the top ten for 1/30 events (3.33%). This exposes a
  retrieval weakness; the 83.33% figure must not be described as accuracy.
- Combining direct source joins with lexical candidates yields 260 unique
  event/article rows and a path for all 30 events in this case. The two paths
  answer different needs: direct links preserve source traceability while
  lexical candidates broaden context.
- Rule-based sector labels are weak: 226/230 lexical candidates are Unknown;
  four map to Energy or Industrials. These are not verified exposures.
- Nifty 50 returns are available as broad descriptive market context for 1, 3,
  7, 14, and 30 trading days after the event. There is no sector-matched or
  causal event estimate.
- The actor-only graph contains 9 nodes and 10 edges.
- Candidate price baskets for both cases are recorded in
  `results/datasets/case_exposure_proxy_register.csv`. This is a transparent
  researcher-defined proxy register, not a GeoSignal-produced or validated
  event-to-security mapping; every row currently has `validated_exposure=false`.

## Additional feasibility case: semiconductor export-controls news

A second replay now uses the archived 2024-03-12 GDELT/GKG data and a VOA
report about the US assessing expanded chip export controls affecting China's
military. Seven GDELT event-code records share that report URL; the same-day
GKG corpus has 120,718 metadata rows and one row with the exact article URL.
Joining by normalized source URL therefore yields seven event-to-one-article
links. These repeated event codes do not represent seven separate articles.

The lexical retrieval result is weak. The exact article ranks 4,033rd in a
same-day TF-IDF ranking from the baseline query. Adding explicit semiconductor,
advanced-chip, export-control, and Commerce Department terms improves it to
rank 55, still outside the top 50. A single-analyst review of the baseline top
20 found 12 unrelated items and 8 adjacent context items, with no directly
relevant article. Most judgements use URL title/slug and GKG metadata rather
than full article text, so this is a diagnostic sample, not a robust precision
estimate. The exact URL path successfully preserves source traceability even
when lexical metadata retrieval fails.

## Matched market-response outputs

Public adjusted-close histories were downloaded for candidate baskets and
benchmarks. Each basket is researcher-defined for evaluation and is not a
sector/security prediction produced by the pipeline. A 120-session market
model was fit before each event; 1, 3, 7, 14, and 30 trading-day descriptive
windows were calculated.

- Red Sea, 2024-01-12: the ports/logistics candidate basket CAR was -1.77% at
  1 day, -0.11% at 7 days, and +5.58% at 30 days. The energy basket was
  +0.58%, +3.90%, and +17.78% for those same windows. These are descriptive
  single-case estimates with no causal attribution or significance test.
- Chip export controls, 2024-03-12: the global semiconductor candidate basket
  CAR was +1.36% at 1 day, -5.08% at 7 days, and -13.94% at 30 days. The
  relevant same-day article did not enter the top 50 metadata-only lexical
  candidates; basket membership was not generated by GeoSignal.

Together, the runs show that historical inputs can be parsed, direct-source
links can be retained, and matched price series can be analysed. They do not
yet show reliable semantic retrieval, validated event-to-sector/security
mapping, predictive stock impact, or causal response. That distinction should
shape the paper's central claim.

## Evidence ready for drafting

You can start writing the live-use motivation and proposed architecture, the
offline historical replay method, the data sources and event selection, the
direct-linkage results, the retrieval diagnostic (including its failure), and
the descriptive market calculations. State that the replay tests whether key
parts of the proposed workflow can be exercised on archived data; do not state
that the architecture is validated for live operation, semantic accuracy, or
reliable stock-impact prediction.

Do not yet write positive claims that the current NLP step reliably finds
relevant reporting, that GeoSignal itself identified the candidate sectors or
securities, or that the reported CARs were caused by either event. The current
pipeline outputs provide little support for those claims: Red Sea rule-based
labels are overwhelmingly Unknown, and the chip-controls direct article ranks
55th after query enrichment and 4,033rd with the baseline query.

## Paper-readiness checklist

The repository now has enough bounded evidence to begin drafting the system
motivation, architecture, offline replay protocol, dataset description, URL
linkage findings, weak retrieval result, and limitations. Before claiming a
promising end-to-end result, add: a pre-defined multi-event evaluation set;
independently reviewed relevance labels; an explicit exposure taxonomy with
reviewed security links; more event windows and appropriate benchmark
comparisons; and uncertainty/significance analysis. If these are not completed
before submission, frame market results as illustrative feasibility outputs,
not validation of event impact.

## Additional evaluation needed for stronger system-level conclusions

- Manually review a stratified sample of candidate event/news pairs to measure
  semantic relevance independently of URL identity. The small Case B audit is
  an initial diagnostic only; it needs a documented sampling plan and at least
  two independent reviewers for a defensible relevance estimate.
- Improve and evaluate event-to-sector/security mapping with an explicit,
  reproducible exposure taxonomy and labelled cases.
- Add post-event histories for matched sector indices or securities before
  reporting market-response results. Exploratory matched-basket price outputs
  now exist for two cases, but the baskets are researcher-defined and are not
  model predictions.
- Replay multiple event types and dates across the cached Q1 archive to test
  repeatability beyond the single Red Sea case.
- Do not report Granger/prediction, ablations, empirical Leontief propagation,
  product/consumer outcomes, or live-system reliability until those evaluations
  have actually been run with suitable labelled data.

## Reproducible artifacts

From the repository root:

- `python scripts/run_redsea_case.py` regenerates the 2024-01-12 case outputs
  when the project dependencies are installed.
- `python scripts/evaluate_redsea_retrieval.py` evaluates the saved case outputs
  against exact source-URL links and writes the event audit, hybrid pair table,
  and metric summary.

The evaluation artifacts are in `results/datasets/`; the market context and
actor graph are alongside the case outputs. Q1 source ZIPs remain in
`data/raw/gdelt/` and `data/raw/gkg/`. Older generated processed Parquet tables
with incorrect date coverage remain recoverable under
`data/processed/archive_stale_20261005/` and must not be cited as Q1 results.
